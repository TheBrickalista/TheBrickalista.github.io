We designed and built a device for cyclic uniaxial mechanical stretching of cells in tissue culture, constructed entirely from LEGO® parts {% cite boulter2020cyclic %}. The system provides a low-cost, accessible way to apply controlled, repeatable mechanical stimulation to cells while they are imaged or otherwise assayed, without requiring specialized commercial equipment.

To make the device reproducible in other labs, we published a detailed step-by-step protocol covering its construction and use, including the accompanying stretchable culture vessel {% cite boulter2021cyclic %}.

This work is part of a broader interest in LEGO®-based instruments as a route to open, frugal science: building scientific devices from an accessible, standardized, widely available parts ecosystem to lower the barrier to entry for labs with limited budgets {% cite boulter2022lego %}. The same approach has since been extended to other instruments developed in the lab, such as Magzy, a LEGO®-based magnetic tweezers system.
