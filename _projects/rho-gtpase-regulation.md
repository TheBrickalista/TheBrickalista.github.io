Between 2002 and 2012, during my PhD and postdoctoral work (including a postdoctoral fellowship at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill), I worked on the regulation of Rho GTPases, small molecular switches that control cytoskeletal organization and cell adhesion.

At UNC, this work centered on RhoGDI1, a regulator of the Rho GTPase cycle. We showed that RhoGDI1 controls the crosstalk, degradation and activity of Rho GTPases {% cite boulter2010regulation %}, with a companion review discussing this regulatory mechanism {% cite garciamata2011invisible %} and a commentary putting it in the context of the broader Rho GTPase field {% cite boulter2010rhogdi %}. We also reviewed alternative, non-canonical mechanisms of Rho GTPase regulation {% cite boulter2012off %} and described experimental approaches to study RhoGDI1-dependent degradation of Rho GTPases {% cite boulter2012analysis %}. Related work from the same lab addressed the nuclear localization and activation of RhoA {% cite dubash2011small %}, the role of RhoG in endothelial cells {% cite vanbuul2007rhog %}, and the structure and function of focal adhesions {% cite dubash2009chapter %}.

Earlier, during my PhD, I worked on integrin-linked kinase (ILK) and its role in cell-matrix adhesion dynamics, including its regulation of Rac-1 {% cite boulter2006regulation %}, its function as part of a modular signaling platform {% cite boulter2006integrin %}, and its requirement for the assembly of matrix-forming adhesions in endothelial cells {% cite vouretcraviari2004ilk %}. This period also included work on Rho GTPase and Src signalling downstream of thrombin and sphingosine-1-phosphate in endothelial cells {% cite vouretcraviari2002distinct %}.

This earlier work laid the foundations of my current interest in RhoA regulation.
